# v0.416.0 - the customer can finally tell the shop what is wrong

## v0.416.0 - the customer can finally tell you what is wrong

**When a customer pressed "Request support from a technician", their own words reached you
through no channel at all.** Every such ticket arrived looking like this:

    Subject: Support requested from DESKTOP-J4K2P1
    Body:    The customer asked for help from this PC.
             Machine:       DESKTOP-J4K2P1
             Reachability:  internet (via the shop server) + LAN 192.168.1.44:8791

You opened a high-priority ticket knowing a machine had asked for help and nothing else. If
the customer had a portal account they could type into that instead, but most do not - and
the one-click tray item is the path a customer with no account uses.

The plumbing for this has existed since the feature was written: the request has always
carried a field for the customer's message, and the server has always rendered it into the
ticket under a **What they said** heading. Nothing ever filled it in.

### What the customer sees now

Pressing the tray item asks two questions before anything else happens:

- **What is going wrong?** - a plain box, no format, no required length.
- **When did it start?** - today, the last few days, longer ago, or "I am not sure",
  because not knowing is a real answer and a box that omits it records a guess as a fact.

Underneath, a panel shows them **the exact message that will be sent**, updating as they
type, and states plainly what else travels with it - the name of this PC and how you reach
it - so nothing about the request is a surprise afterwards.

**Cancelling arms nothing and sends nothing.** The question is asked *before* remote access
is armed, deliberately: a customer who opens it and changes their mind should not discover
they have left the PC reachable as a side effect of a dialog they closed. Previously the
machine was armed first and the customer was asked nothing at all.

### What you see now

**The ticket's subject is the customer's own first line**, not the machine name. A queue of
twenty tickets all reading "Support requested from DESKTOP-..." tells you nothing about
which to open first, and the machine is already on the ticket's Device field - the subject
was spending itself twice on the same fact. Their whole message is in the ticket body under
**What they said**.

A PC on an older console sends no words, and its tickets keep the old machine-named subject
exactly as before. Nothing gets worse by not updating.

**Pressing the button twice does not open two tickets.** A repeat within a minute hands back
the ticket the customer already has rather than a second one - and it is never a refusal,
because a request for help must not be dropped. A *correction* is not a repeat: a customer
who says "the screen is black" and then realises thirty seconds later that it is the
keyboard gets that second message recorded, rather than silently swallowed.

### Worth knowing

**The customer's message is length-bounded** before it is stored. It lands in a ticket you
read, and an authenticated device could otherwise have pushed a very large amount of text
into your Service Desk.

**Nothing here has run on real hardware**, as with the previous three releases. The dialog is
constructed and driven by the build on the real Windows runtime, and the server behaviour is
proven against a real server over a real connection, but no customer has yet pressed the
button on a real PC.
