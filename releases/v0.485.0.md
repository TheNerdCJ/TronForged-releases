# v0.485.0 - TronForged 0.485.0 - SSD secure erase, account sign-in and connection fixes

## TronForged 0.485.0 - SSD secure erase, account sign-in and connection fixes

### Secure Data Erasure (SSD and firmware erase)

- A firmware erase is no longer certified on the drive's word alone. Before the drive erases itself, TronForged reads 64
  evenly spaced samples of the drive (4 MiB each) and reads them again afterwards. If any sample that held data still
  holds exactly the same data, the drive is reported NOT erased, even though the drive said it finished. The certificate
  now names the sampled check - how many samples and how many bytes - next to the drive's own confirmation.
- If every sample was already blank before the erase, the certificate says the sampled check was inconclusive rather than
  claiming it passed.
- An NVMe drive that was sanitized before keeps reporting "sanitize completed". That old report is no longer accepted as
  proof of a new sanitize: the drive's status is read before the command is sent, and the erase is confirmed only when
  the status changes, the drive reports the sanitize in progress, or the samples show the data changed.
- A SATA SSD in a USB dock is now recognised as an SSD (from the drive's own identity, not the dock's). Its recommended
  method, the order of the methods, the SSD warning on the page and the SSD note on the certificate are now correct.
- Each SSD in the drive list now says whether its firmware erase is available, frozen by the BIOS, blocked by the
  enclosure, or not supported - before you choose a method. Checking only asks the drive; it changes nothing.
- The method chosen for you follows that answer: the strongest firmware erase the drive can run (Enhanced Secure Erase or
  NVMe crypto erase when the drive has it, otherwise Secure Erase). When a firmware erase cannot run, an overwrite is
  chosen and one line says why.
- A drive whose firmware erase is frozen by the BIOS now has a "Sleep this PC to unlock the drive" button. It asks
  first, puts the PC to sleep, and checks the drives again when the PC wakes. It is never offered for the drive Windows
  runs from, or while an erase is running.
- A firmware erase cannot be stopped once the drive has started it. Its row no longer shows a Stop button that did
  nothing; it says "The drive is erasing itself; this cannot be stopped. Do not unplug it."
- While an NVMe Sanitize runs, its row now shows the drive's own progress percentage.
- Firmware erase has still not been run on a real SSD at our bench: check the first drives you erase this way.

### Signing in to your shop account

- The Home page of the customer console now has a "Your account" box near the top. Choose
  "Sign in to your account" to sign in to the account your repair shop set up for you - your
  tickets, invoices and messages. It is the same sign-in as before (Settings and the icon near
  the clock still open it too), with your password and, if your shop uses it, the code from your
  authenticator app.
- No account yet? The box shows the shop's phone number - ask them to set one up.
- Forgot your password? Choose "Forgot your password?" in the box to see the shop's phone
  number. The shop resets passwords and authenticator codes.
- Once you are signed in, the box says who you are signed in as, with "Open my account" and
  "Sign out". Signing out also ends the session at the shop, not just on this PC.
- This PC now remembers the user name you last signed in with successfully, and fills it in for
  you next time. It keeps only the user name - never your password or a code. If it is not your
  name, choose "Not you?" to clear it.
- Disconnecting this PC from the shop, or uninstalling the program, removes the remembered name.

### Connecting to the shop

- When this PC cannot connect to the shop and both the shop's own port and a web port answered
  but then failed partway, the message now adds: "If this keeps happening, restart this PC and
  check its security software, VPN or Windows updates, then ask the shop." This can happen right
  after a big Windows update. The details about each port are still shown.

### Licence and privacy

- The licence (section 11.6) now says how a firmware erase is confirmed: the drive must report it finished and a
  sample of the drive, read before and after, must show the data changed. A sampled check reads only the samples it
  names, not the whole drive. It also says the Software can, after you confirm, put the computer to sleep to release a
  drive whose firmware erase the BIOS has frozen - save your work first.
- The privacy notice (section 4.1) now says the customer console remembers, on that PC only, the user name of the
  last successful sign-in to your account - never a password or a code - and that "Not you?", disconnecting the PC
  from the shop or uninstalling removes it.
