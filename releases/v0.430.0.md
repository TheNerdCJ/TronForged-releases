# v0.430.0 - Your customers can answer you, and AI can help you reply

## v0.430.0 - Your customers can answer you, and AI can help you reply

> **This release includes v0.425.0 through v0.429.0**, none of which were published on
their own. If you are updating from v0.424.0, everything below is new to you.

### Before you update: your server must be on the embedded database

PostgreSQL is retired in this release. The embedded database has been the default since
v0.368.0, and a server that is still set to PostgreSQL will **refuse to start** after this
update. Nothing is changed and no data is touched when it refuses.

To see which one your server uses, open **Server Console > Set up server > Database engine**.
If it still runs on PostgreSQL, move it BEFORE you update: on that same page, click
**Rehearse the move...** (it changes nothing), then **Move to the embedded store...**.

From a command line the equivalent is: stop the service, run
`TronForged.Server.exe --import-pg --verify-only --data <dir>` to rehearse, then
`--import-pg --data <dir>`, then `--set-store sqlite --data <dir>`, clear the service
dependency with `sc config TronForgedServer depend= /`, and start the service.

If a server does refuse to start, Windows may still show the service as Running. The reason
is shown under **Server Console > Set up server > Database engine**, next to *Running on*.

**If your consoles cannot reach the server after it updates:** open the Server Console on
the server itself and press **Update everything on this PC**. Your data is intact. This
update changes the database, so the previous version can no longer open it, but this one can.

### Your customers can read your reply and answer it

The customer console has a **Messages from your shop** window. It shows the jobs for that
PC, plus any job on the same customer's record that is not tied to one particular PC: jobs
booked at the counter or by phone, and jobs raised before your server reached v0.424.0. It
shows the whole conversation on each and a box to write back. If a customer's record covers
several PCs - a business, for example - every one of those PCs can see and answer those
shared jobs.

It needs **no account** - it uses the PC's own connection to your server, exactly like the
support button. Three ways in: the tray menu, a card in the customer's **Settings** tab, and
clicking the "your repair shop replied" notice, which used to do nothing. Your internal notes
and attachments never appear there.

A ticket the customer has answered, and nobody has answered back, gets its own line on the
**Needs attention today** card, so a customer writing to you can no longer make their job
*less* visible.

Limits: a message longer than 4,000 characters is cut to its first 4,000, a PC may send 20
messages an hour, and a conversation shows its newest 50 messages and says so when older ones
are hidden.

### A customer writing on a closed job now reopens it

Until now, a customer who replied to a ticket you had already **closed** - from their own PC
or from their shop account - had their message saved where nobody at the shop would see it:
closed tickets are hidden from the Service Desk by default and skipped by the *Needs
attention today* card. A customer reply to a closed ticket now reopens it, exactly as a reply
to a resolved one already did, so it lands in front of you. A "thanks!" on a finished job
will reopen it too; close it again when you have read it.

### Draft a reply or a resolution with AI

The Service Desk reply box now has a **Draft a reply with AI** button, and the Resolve window
has **Rewrite with AI**. Technicians see these buttons whether or not the feature is on; they
only work once **Draft a reply or resolution** is ticked (it starts OFF, and is separate from
*Brief a ticket*). Otherwise they explain how to turn it on.

The draft goes into the box for you to read and edit - **nothing is sent until you send it.**

What it sends to your AI provider: that ticket's subject and **only the messages your
customer can already see on it**. Your internal notes are left out of what is sent, not
merely left unmentioned. For a resolution it also sends the same summary of recorded work
the Resolve window already fills in for you.

**Your customer is told.** When you send a message that started as an AI draft, your server
adds this line to the end of it:

> (Written with the help of an AI assistant, and sent by a person at your repair shop.)

It is part of the stored message, so your customer sees it on their own PC, in their
account, and anywhere else the message appears. It is added only to text that came from the
draft button - text someone pastes in from elsewhere cannot be labelled. As with the
work-log draft, **Resolve** stays disabled until you have changed at least something in an
AI-written resolution.

### Brief this ticket

A brief lets a technician pick up a job quickly: what the customer came in with, what has
happened since, and what is still outstanding. Every ticket in the Service Desk now has a
**Brief this ticket** button. It works only while *Brief a ticket* is ticked. The brief
appears above the conversation, labelled as AI-generated, and disappears when you reload.

It is a reading aid, not a diagnosis. It is told not to suggest a cause or a fix, or a
priority or a category, and it cannot change the ticket: nothing it writes is saved.

What it sends: that one ticket's subject and its whole conversation - the customer's
messages, your replies, and **your internal notes on that ticket**. Under the brief, the
console tells you exactly how many messages were sent and whether anything was shortened or
left out. On a very long conversation the oldest messages are the ones left out, never the
latest, and the brief says so.

Only a staff account can ask for a brief. An account you have linked to a customer record
cannot, whatever permissions it has been given.

### Each AI feature now has its own switch

Below the master switch in **Server Console > Settings > AI features** there is now a list
headed *What this shop's AI may be used for*, with one tick per feature and, beside each, a
plain sentence saying what that feature sends.

- **Analyze fleet health**, **Draft a customer summary** and **Analyze server sizing** stay
  ON for a shop that had already enabled AI. Turning AI on was consent to exactly those, and
  this release does not take them away.
- **Brief a ticket** and **Draft a reply or resolution** are new, and both start OFF.

### Passwords typed into tickets

Customers type their Wi-Fi password into a support request more often than anyone would
like. Before a ticket brief or an AI draft is sent, text shaped like a password, an API key,
a BitLocker recovery key or a Windows product key is replaced with `[secret removed]`, and
you are told how many were replaced.

This is a check on the *shape* of the text. It is **not** a guarantee that no secret is
sent: a password that looks like an ordinary word will not be caught.

### A daily limit per person

Each signed-in person may make at most 25 AI requests a day (the day runs midnight to
midnight UTC), across all AI features. The count is kept in the server's database, so
restarting the server - which updates do - does not reset it. Your shop's monthly token
budget still applies on top.

### What your customers see

While Brief a ticket or Draft a reply or resolution is on, the **Messages from your shop**
window on your customers' PCs and their **My shop account** window carry a line telling them
that what they write there may be sent to your AI provider, and for what. Turn both features
off and the line goes away. A customer whose shop does not use them is told nothing, because
nothing is happening to them.

The licence says the same thing: Sections 3.2, 15.1, 15.2, 15.3, 17.2 and 19.1 are updated
in this release.

### A security fix: two PCs with the same name could see each other's notices

For a PC not filed under a customer, the server matched tickets to it by the machine's
**name** - and names are not unique and are chosen by the machine. Two PCs both called
"Reception PC" could be told about each other's tickets, including the customer's own
description of the problem. It is now keyed on the machine's enrolled identity.

One side effect: on a PC that is not filed under a customer, jobs it raised before your
server reached v0.424.0 no longer appear on that PC. File the PC under its customer, or
contact the customer directly, for any such job still open.

### PostgreSQL has been retired

The embedded database has been the default since v0.368.0 and is now the only one. A server
still set to PostgreSQL refuses to start and says how to move, rather than starting and
failing quietly. See *Before you update* at the top for how to move it.

### Also fixed

- **Closing "My shop account" could close the whole customer console.** If a customer closed
  their **My shop account** window while it was still working - signing in, loading their
  tickets or devices, uploading a backup, or saving a file - the customer console itself
  could close with it. The window now simply stops what it was doing.
- **The Service Desk labelled the customer's own messages "SENT TO CUSTOMER".** It has done
  so since customers were first able to write into a ticket. A message the customer wrote
  now says **FROM THE CUSTOMER**.
- **Accented names came back from the AI as question marks.** Accented letters used in
  Western European languages (such as e with an acute, n with a tilde, u with an umlaut) now
  come through, and curly quotes and dashes are shown as plain ones. Letters outside that set
  are still shown as question marks, because that is also where the characters used to
  disguise text live.
- **The summary-draft feature now has the same protection as the others** against text
  that tries to pass itself off as instructions.

### Worth knowing

**Not yet tried against a real model on real tickets.** Everything here is tested, but
whether the brief and the drafts are useful on your actual backlog - and how they handle a
customer who writes instructions into a ticket - is something only real use will show. Read
every draft before you send it. Tell us.
