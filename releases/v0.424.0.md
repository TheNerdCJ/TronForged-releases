## v0.424.0 - a ticket knows which machine it is about

Open a ticket and it now tells you about the PC: when it was last seen, whether it is
behaving like itself, the last service run, open alerts, and outstanding Windows updates.

### The ticket records the machine that asked for help

When a customer presses the support button on their PC, that request already proves which
machine it came from - the PC signs in with its own credential to make it. **From this
release the ticket keeps that**, so a technician opening it knows exactly which machine to
look at rather than reading a machine name somebody typed.

Any other ticket - a walk-in, one raised at the counter - can be pointed at a machine by
hand: **Choose machine...** offers that customer's own enrolled PCs and nobody else's.

**This is not the same thing as the asset record, and a ticket can carry both.** The
MACHINE is a PC in your fleet that you manage and can connect to. The ASSET is the
customer's own property at the counter - a games console, a phone, a TV - that will never
run an agent. Setting one does not touch the other.

### The silences are the feature

Five facts are shown, always, whether or not there is an answer. That sounds like a small
thing and it is the whole point of the release.

A fact the shop cannot answer **says why** instead of going blank:

- *"This PC has never checked for Windows updates."*
- *"Not enough history yet to say what normal looks like for this PC."*
- *"This PC has never sent any readings, so there is nothing to compare."*

And a fact **your own account is not allowed to see** says that separately, in a different
colour, so you know to ask an administrator rather than to go and look at the PC. A
reception login sees the ticket and the customer's words, and is not handed every machine's
unpatched-vulnerability count - that was a deliberate line to draw.

**Two sentences this release makes impossible**, both of which the product could have said
before:

- **"Up to date"** about a PC whose update check FAILED. The count is only ever quoted when
  the check actually completed; a failed, blocked or partly-finished scan says so instead.
  A zero from a partial scan means "we found none", not "there are none", and the update
  plane has always known that - the ticket panel now honours it too.
- **"Behaving the way it usually does"** about a PC that has never reported anything. That
  reassurance and "we genuinely cannot tell" used to be the same word internally. They are
  four separate answers now: never reported, not enough history, could not work it out, and
  actually fine.

### Open alerts means YOUR alerts

The number here is the shop's own monitoring alerts for that machine, read per-machine and
in full. It is deliberately **not** the number on the fleet tile, which the customer's PC
works out for itself from two fixed thresholds and stops counting at two. Those are two
different numbers that have been travelling under one word.

### Worth knowing

**Nothing on your server needs doing.** No database change, and no existing ticket is
altered.

**Tickets raised before this release will not name a machine** - there was nothing
recording it. They say so plainly and offer the picker; new support requests fill it in by
themselves.

**Not yet tried on real hardware.** The panel and the picker are built and tested against a
real database, but a live fleet with a mix of reporting and silent machines is still on the
bench list.
