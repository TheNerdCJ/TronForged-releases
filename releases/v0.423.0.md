## v0.423.0 - a ticket says what was done, and Ctrl+Alt+Delete actually works now

Two things: a fix to what v0.422.0 shipped yesterday, and the next step in the ticketing
work.

### First, the fix: Ctrl+Alt+Delete was dead on most machines

v0.422.0 added the Ctrl+Alt+Delete button and switched on the Windows setting it needs -
but only when somebody clicked **Install helper** by hand. On a PC whose full-control
helper was already working, which is nearly all of them, nothing ever switched it on. You
would have updated, seen the new button, pressed it, and been told **"Ctrl+Alt+Delete: not
permitted on this PC"** - and it would have said that for ever.

That was a mistake in how the fix was fitted, not in the idea. The setting now belongs to
the helper rather than to one button: it is switched on when the helper is installed, when
it is repaired, and by the fifteen-minute health check that keeps it running. **Your fleet
fixes itself.** Each PC sorts itself out within about fifteen minutes of updating, with
nobody clicking anything.

Everything else about it is unchanged and still true: whatever the setting was before is
recorded, removing the helper puts it back, a PC that already permitted it is left alone,
and an accessibility tool that was already allowed to send the sequence keeps working.

**If a machine still says "not permitted" an hour after it updated**, a group policy on
your network is setting it back - that is worth a word with whoever manages it, rather
than clicking Install helper.

### Resolving a ticket now tells the customer something

**"Resolved" used to be one value in the Status list.** One click, no words. A ticket could
be closed out having never told the customer what was wrong or what you did - and your own
record of the repair was whatever somebody happened to type into the thread, which on a
busy day is nothing at all.

It is a step now. Beside the Status list there is a **Resolve...** button, and it asks for
one paragraph the customer will read.

- **"Done" is refused by name**, and so are "Fixed", "Sorted", "N/A" and the rest. So is
  anything too short to be useful. The box says which, while you type.
- **The box is pre-filled** with what was actually run on that ticket, so you are editing
  rather than starting from nothing.
- **But the pre-filled text is not an answer**, and the button knows it: **Resolve** stays
  greyed until you have added something of your own. A list of tool names is a record of
  what was done, not an explanation of what was wrong, and only a person can write the
  second one.

The paragraph goes onto the ticket as a message the customer reads, and the ticket records
who resolved it and when. If the customer replies afterwards the ticket reopens and that
stamp is cleared - it should not go on naming a technician for work that is open again.

**This is the only way in.** The old route is refused, so an older console cannot slip a
ticket into "resolved" without the paragraph.

### What the shop ran is now recorded on the ticket

Open a ticket and click **Log my work here**. From then on every fix and every automated
service run you start is recorded against that ticket: what ran, **what it was observed to
do**, on which machine, by whom. The strip at the top of the Service Desk confirms each one
- and says so just as loudly if a record did not reach the server.

Two deliberate choices worth knowing:

- **It is shop-internal.** A customer never sees a work record and cannot write one. What
  they see is the resolution paragraph, which a person writes.
- **Nothing is recorded until you click the button.** The product does not guess which
  ticket your work belongs to. A work record on the wrong ticket is a lie in your own
  history - and a resolution may later be written from it.

### Worth knowing

**Nothing on your server needs doing.** The database update applies itself on the next
start and changes no existing row.

**Your existing resolved tickets are untouched.** They keep their status, and they show it;
the Status list simply no longer offers "resolved" as something to pick.
