# TronForged v0.490.0

Remote Desktop (RDP) through TronForged for PCs with no monitor, and smoother remote desktop video on graphics cards that support AV1.

## Remote Desktop (RDP) through TronForged

A remote desktop to a Windows Server with no monitor used to show no picture: with no screen attached, Windows has
nothing to capture. Now you can use Windows' own Remote Desktop instead, through your TronForged session. Windows gives
a Remote Desktop session its own screen, monitor or not, so you get a full desktop at your window's size.

- **Where:** on Windows PCs (Pro, Enterprise, Education and Server) in your fleet, open the Remote Desktop window, take
  control, and choose **Commands > Open Remote Desktop (RDP)**. Windows' own Remote Desktop client opens on your PC.
  It works at the sign-in screen and on a server nobody is signed in to.
- **Your sign-in stays with Microsoft:** you sign in with a Windows account of that PC in Microsoft's own window.
  TronForged never sees, asks for or stores that password.
- **Nothing opened to the internet:** the connection travels inside the encrypted TronForged session. Your console
  listens only on your own PC (127.0.0.2), and the customer PC connects only to its own Remote Desktop - it cannot be
  pointed at any other computer or port. No port forwarding, and the firewall is never changed.
- **No certificate prompt:** the customer PC reports the certificate its Remote Desktop really uses, through the
  encrypted session, and Windows' client is told to trust exactly that certificate for that one window. If the PC ever
  presents a different certificate, Windows still warns you.
- **Turning it on:** if Remote Desktop is off on that PC, TronForged offers to turn it on and first lists exactly what
  changes: Remote Desktop connections allowed, and Network Level Authentication required (it is never lowered).
  **Turn Remote Desktop off again...** in the same menu puts it back. Remember to turn it off when the work is done.
- **Who:** only the technician in control. A technician watching read-only is not offered it, and neither is a
  one-time support session.
- **Windows Home** has no Remote Desktop host, so the menu says so there - use the normal remote desktop window.
- **One person at a time on Pro, Enterprise and Education:** a Remote Desktop sign-in locks the person at the screen out
  of their session. Warn them first.
- **Licensing:** Windows Server allows two administrative Remote Desktop sessions without extra licences. More need
  Microsoft Remote Desktop Services CALs, which are the shop's to buy.
- **On the record:** the customer PC's remote audit log and the shop server's audit log record who opened Remote
  Desktop, when, for how long and how many bytes crossed each way, and every time it was turned on or off. Never what
  was on the screen or typed.

Both PCs and the shop server need this version. With an older one nothing new appears and nothing else changes.

## AV1 video on the graphics card

Remote desktop can now send moving pictures - a video playing, a photo or a window being dragged - as AV1 video, made by
the customer PC's graphics card, where both PCs support it. Text, menus and scrolling pages keep the sharp picture tiles
they use today. Everywhere else it works exactly as before.

## Remote desktop

- **Video only where video helps.** For every frame, TronForged looks at what changed on the customer's screen. Text,
  windows and menus - and scrolling a page - are sent as picture tiles, exactly as before. Moving pictures are sent as
  AV1 video. Only the parts of the screen that changed are replaced, so text next to a video stays sharp.
- **The picture sharpens when it stops.** About a second after the screen goes still, the parts that were sent as video
  are sent once more as picture tiles, so text that was under a video or a dragged window is sharp again.
- **Made by the graphics card.** The customer PC's graphics card encodes the video in hardware (for example NVIDIA
  GeForce RTX 40-series, Intel Arc and recent Intel Core with Xe graphics, AMD Radeon RX 7000-series), and your PC
  decodes it. TronForged never encodes video in software, so it does not load the customer's processor.
- **Nothing to install, nothing to switch on.** A PC whose graphics card cannot encode AV1 - most servers, and most
  older PCs - keeps the picture tiles for the whole session. So does a session from a console that cannot decode AV1.
- **Nothing changes when either end is older.** Video is used only when your console and the customer's PC (and, before
  sign-in, the TronForged helper there) are all v0.490.0 or later. Older consoles and helpers keep the picture tiles.
- **The customer's pointer still shows** on top of the picture, exactly as before.

## Known limits

- AV1 video has not been tried yet on real customer hardware or over real customer connections. It has been tested on a
  development PC that has an AV1 decoder but no AV1 graphics card, so the video itself was not measured there.
- The first moment of video in a session costs more data than the rest (the video has to start with a complete picture).
- While it is moving, a picture sent as video is softer than picture tiles; it sharpens about a second after it stops.
- Very large desktops (several screens side by side, wider than 4096 pixels) keep the picture tiles.
- The remote desktop's statistics overlay (F12) does not show yet when video is being used.
