## v0.419.0 - updates no longer depend on your backup drive

### The fault a shop reported

"Pull the latest release from GitHub" answered:

    The network path was not found. : '\\192.168.1.8\Backups\rel3c584b7b...'

The download had worked. The **server could not store what it had fetched**, because
downloaded releases were being written into the shop's **backup destination** - and that
share was not reachable. Every automatic pull had been failing the same way for weeks: the
shop's catalogue was stuck while their own console had moved on by nine releases.

There was never a reason for it. Releases went into the backup store because that was the
storage the server already had, not because they belong there. The consequences all ran
one way: the ability to update your whole fleet depended on a drive that has nothing to do
with updating, a NAS switched off for maintenance stopped the pipeline, and changing your
backup location silently invalidated every release the server had stored.

**Releases now live on the server's own disk**, beside its database, where they cannot be
affected by the backup destination at all. This finishes a move the product already made
once: the database's daily snapshot was taken off the backup destination in v0.375.0 for a
neighbouring reason, and releases were simply left behind.

**Nothing is lost when you update.** Releases already stored on your backup share are
still read from there, so your existing catalogue keeps working. New pulls go to the
server's own disk.

**And it now fails before the download, not after it.** The check that the storage is
reachable used to happen last, so an unreachable destination cost a 50 MB transfer and
then produced a raw Windows error naming a path. It happens first now, and says which
destination is at fault and where to fix it.

### The AI training data could never record a crash

The anomaly plane exports a daily per-machine record so a future model can learn which
machines are heading for trouble. Three of its columns are the "label" - the failures a
machine actually saw that day.

Examined against a real export - 82 days of records across seven machines - **the crash
column was zero in every single row**. Not because nothing crashed: because a crash was
recorded only in the deduplicated crash list the digest reads, and never as a dated event,
so that column was incapable of being anything else. Any model trained on it would have
learned that machines never crash, from data in which crashing could not be expressed.

A crash is now recorded both ways: as the deduplicated entry your digest shows, and as a
dated event the daily record can count. The two are different shapes of the same crash on
purpose - deduplication deliberately loses the per-day detail, which is exactly what the
daily record needs.

**This only helps from here on.** Records already exported cannot gain labels
retrospectively.

### Worth knowing

**Nothing you interact with has changed** - no screen, no setting, no stored data moved on
your machine. Existing releases stay where they are and keep working.
